How to play

Literature (also called Canadian Fish, or just Fish) is all about questions, memory, and deduction.

The goal

You and two teammates collect complete sets which are called half-suits or books. Eight sets are in play. Capture more than the other team and you win.

You win a set as a team. Cards keep moving, so a set belongs to the team once all six sit on your side — in one hand or spread across partners.

The eight sets

Strip the four 8s from a 52-card deck. Forty-eight left. Each suit splits in two: low 2–7 and high 9–A. Each player is dealt 8 cards. Never show your hand, not even to partners.

Teams and seating

Two teams of three, sitting alternately: A1 — B1 — A2 — B2 — A3 — B3 (and B3 sits next to A1). Everyone sits between two opponents. The host can keep that split, randomize it, or move people; both sides stay three-and-three.

You cannot plan out loud or show cards. All communication is the questions you ask, the ones you skip and answers to those question in terms of card movements.

Your turn

Ask one opponent for one exact card which you don't hold but have another card from that half-suit.

Example

Your only spade is the Q♠. You may ask for the 9♠ 10♠ J♠ K♠ or A♠ — not the Queen, and not any low spade.

What happens next

  • Hit. They pass it face-up. You add it to your hand and ask again until you miss.
  • Miss. The turn passes to the person you asked.

Claiming a set

When your team has all six, claim on your turn. Hold them all: lay down. Spread across partners: assign who has which. Then one of three things happens:

  • Correct. Your team keeps the set. Those cards leave play.
  • Wrong split. You had them all, but named the wrong hands. The set is dead. Nobody scores.
  • Opponents held at least one card. The set goes to the other team.

Example: you hold 2, 3, 4, 6, 7♥. Doug earlier asked for 3♥, so he holds a low heart — the 5. You say: “Claim low hearts — Doug has the 5, I have the rest.” Correct: you score.

You keep the turn after. If it emptied your hand, pass to a teammate with cards.

Ending and winning

The game ends when all 8 sets are claimed or forfeited. A dead set belongs to no one, so 4–3 is possible.

Player with no cards are done for the game, they can’t be asked and thus can't ask. After a claim if you become empty: choose a teammate with cards. When a whole team is out, the other team must claim what’s left.

Reading the table

  • Anyone may only ask what the last question and answer were.
  • At any point, one may ask how many cards a player holds. Answer truthfully.
  • Claimed sets are public.
  • No notes.

“A asked for 9♥” means A holds a high heart, not the 9. If it hits B, A now has the 9 and B is lighter. Stack those facts.

Strategy

From game one

  • Track every question. Who, what, yes or no.
  • Drop dead sets. If you can’t ask in it, forget it.
  • Don’t claim on a hunch. Sure, then prompt.

Next

  • Safe sets can’t be stolen. Lock other sets first.
  • Ask to inform. A miss can tell partners you hold that set.
  • Starve a dangerous opponent of turns. A miss hands them the ask.
  • Skip the tug-of-war. Trading one card leaks the whole table.
