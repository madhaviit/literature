How to play

Six players. Two teams of three. Ask an opponent for one card. The side with more sets wins.

The goal

There are 8 sets. A set scores for the team that holds all six cards, in one hand or split among the three partners. Most sets wins.

Ask opponents only. Never a partner.

The eight sets

Take the 8s out of a 52-card deck. 48 cards remain. Each suit is two sets: low 2-7 and high 9-A.

  • ♠ low: 2♠3♠4♠5♠6♠7♠
  • ♠ high: 9♠10♠J♠Q♠K♠A♠
  • ♥ low: 2♥3♥4♥5♥6♥7♥
  • ♥ high: 9♥10♥J♥Q♥K♥A♥
  • ♦ low: 2♦3♦4♦5♦6♦7♦
  • ♦ high: 9♦10♦J♦Q♦K♦A♦
  • ♣ low: 2♣3♣4♣5♣6♣7♣
  • ♣ high: 9♣10♣J♣Q♣K♣A♣

Deal 8 cards each. Do not show your hand.

Teams and seating

Teams of three, seated alternately: A, B, A, B, A, B. Each player sits between two opponents. The host sets the teams before the deal. Both sides stay at three.

No talk about the cards. No showing hands.

Your turn

On your turn, ask one opponent for one card. The ask is legal only if:

  1. You name the exact card. “Seven of spades,” not “a spade.”
  2. You hold another card of that set.
  3. You do not already hold the card you name.
  4. They are an opponent, and they still have cards.

Example

Your only spade is the Q♠. You may ask for the 9♠ 10♠ J♠ K♠ or A♠. Not the Queen, and not any low spade.

What happens next

  • Yes. They give it to you face up. You ask again.
  • No. They take the turn. You hold that set, but not that card. They do not hold it.

Claiming a set

On your turn, if your side holds all six, claim the set. If you hold all six, lay them down. If partners hold some, name who has each card.

  • Right. Your side scores the set. Those cards are out.
  • Wrong names. Your side had them, but you named the wrong hands. The set is dead. Nobody scores.
  • They held one. The other side scores it.

The six cards are shown. They go to the side that scored, or out of play if the set is dead. You may not write the layout down.

Example. You hold 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 of hearts. Doug asked for the 3 earlier, so he holds the 5. Claim low hearts: Doug has the 5, you have the rest.

You keep the turn after a claim. If your hand is empty, name a partner who still has cards.

Ending and winning

The hand ends when all 8 sets are scored or dead. Most sets wins. 4-4 is a draw. A dead set scores for neither side, so 4-3 is possible.

You cannot ask a player with no cards. If your hand is empty, choose a partner who still has cards. If your whole side is out, the other side must claim what is left. No more asks.

Reading the table

  • Anyone may ask what the last question and answer were.
  • Anyone may ask how many cards a player holds. Answer honestly.
  • Scored sets stay face up, including dead ones.
  • No written notes.

If A asks for the 9 of hearts, A holds a high heart other than the 9. If B has it, A takes the 9 and B has one card fewer.

Strategy

Count

  • Remember each ask: who, which card, yes or no.
  • If you cannot ask in a set, stop tracking it.
  • Claim only when you can name every card.

Order

  • A set you hold outright cannot be taken. Clear the others first.
  • A miss tells your partners which set you hold.
  • A no gives that opponent the next ask.

Partners

  • Do not trade the same card back and forth. Each ask shows the set.
  • Partner misses the 2 of hearts and you hold it: ask a different low heart.
  • You do not hold it: ask for the 2 yourself. You are void in the set: change sets.
